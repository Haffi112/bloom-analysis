Annotator instructions

What you are doing
 
Each item is a multiple-choice question from a machine-learning course exam. The questions were generated by a language
 model, which also assigned each one a level in Bloom's taxonomy. We want to know whether people agree with
 those assignments. You will not see the model's label, the answer statistics, or the other annotator's choices.
 
For every question, decide which cognitive process a student must perform to arrive at the correct answer.
 You are classifying the thinking demanded, not how hard or how long the question is, and not how important the topic is.

The six levels (revised Bloom taxonomy, Anderson & Krathwohl 2001)
 
Levels are ordered from least to most cognitively demanding. Typical verbs and a multiple-choice cue are given for each.

1 · Remember
 
Retrieve knowledge from memory. Recognise or recall a definition, fact, name, or standard formula.
 
Verbs: define, list, name, recall, recognise, identify, state.
 
MCQ cue: the correct option is essentially the textbook phrase; a student who memorised the material can answer without
 reasoning about a situation. 
Example: "Which cost function is typically used for multi-label classification?" → binary cross-entropy.

2 · Understand
 
Construct meaning from the material. Explain, paraphrase, summarise, classify, compare, give an example, or infer a consequence.
 
Verbs: explain, describe, interpret, summarise, classify, compare, exemplify, infer.
 
MCQ cue: asks why, what does X mean, how do X and Y differ; options are reworded concepts, so the student
 must grasp the idea, not just recognise words. 
Example: "Why does using a pre-trained network as a feature extractor reduce compute?"

3 · Apply
 
Carry out or use a procedure in a given situation. Execute a known method, calculate, or pick the correct procedure for a concrete case.
 
Verbs: apply, use, execute, implement, compute, solve, demonstrate.
 
MCQ cue: a concrete scenario or numbers are given and the student must run the appropriate rule or method on it.
 
Example: "A model outputs probabilities 0.2 / 0.7 / 0.1 and the threshold is 0.5 — which class is predicted?"

4 · Analyze
 
Break material into parts and determine how they relate to each other and to the whole. Distinguish relevant from
 irrelevant, find the cause, attribute a symptom to a mechanism, diagnose.
 
Verbs: analyse, differentiate, distinguish, diagnose, attribute, organise, detect.
 
MCQ cue: a scenario contains several elements and the student must work out which one matters or what is really going on
 (e.g. spot the data leak, identify the outcome vs. the context in interaction data).
 
Example: "Which scenario best illustrates data leakage caused by the target being a function of a feature?"

5 · Evaluate
 
Make judgements based on criteria and standards. Critique, check for consistency, justify a choice, select the most appropriate option
 among defensible alternatives.
 
Verbs: evaluate, judge, critique, justify, assess, recommend, select (the best).
 
MCQ cue: "Which is the best / most appropriate approach…" where several options are plausible and must be weighed against
 criteria (cost, bias, validity). If only one option is even sensible, it is probably Understand or Apply, not Evaluate.
 
Example: "Which strategy is most appropriate to prevent a recommendation system from learning only from outputs it has already filtered?"

6 · Create
 
Put elements together to form a novel, coherent whole. Design, plan, generate, or construct something new.
 
Verbs: design, plan, construct, generate, formulate, devise.
 
MCQ cue: rare in multiple choice. Use only when the student genuinely has to synthesise a design or plan, not merely recognise one.

How to decide

Read the stem and all four options. Look at the marked correct answer so you know what is being asked for.
 
Ask: what would a student have to do in their head to get this right? Recall a phrase → Remember. Grasp a concept → Understand.
 Run a method on a case → Apply. Pick apart a scenario → Analyze. Weigh alternatives against criteria → Evaluate. Build something new → Create.
 
Pick the highest level that is genuinely required. A question that could be answered by recall alone is Remember even if it is phrased
 as a scenario. Conversely, do not inflate a level because the topic is advanced.
 
Choose the primary level. If you are honestly torn between two, also record the other as secondary.
 Use secondary sparingly — it should mean "I could see it either way", not "this is also somewhat related".
 
Use the comment box for anything that might help resolve a disagreement later (e.g. "stem says 'best' but only one option is sensible").

Please do not

Base the level on how difficult you think the question is, how long it is, or how obscure the topic is.
 
Use the answer explanations as evidence of level — they are LLM-written justifications, shown only for context.
 
Try to guess what the model or the other annotator chose. Independent judgement is the point.
